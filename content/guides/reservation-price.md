Every negotiator needs one number before they start: **the point at which they'll walk away.** That's your reservation price.

## What it is

- For a **buyer**, it's the most you'll pay.
- For a **seller**, it's the least you'll accept.

Any deal better than your reservation price leaves you better off than walking away. Any deal worse leaves you worse off.

## How to calculate yours

Your reservation price should come from your [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna), your best alternative if there's no deal.

**Example: selling a car.** A dealer has offered you £7,000 today. Selling privately takes time, adverts and strangers on your drive, which you value at about £200. So your reservation price for a private sale is around £7,200. Anything above that beats the dealer.

**Example: a job offer.** You earn £45,000 now and like your job. Moving has costs: a longer commute, losing accrued holiday, starting over. You might decide you'd need at least £49,000 to make the move worthwhile. That's your reservation price.

## Estimate theirs too

The overlap between your reservation price and theirs is the [ZOPA](/guides/zopa-negotiation). To estimate theirs:

- research what their alternatives cost
- look at comparable deals
- ask open questions about their situation and timing

## Never reveal it

"My budget is £15,000" is the most expensive sentence in negotiation. The quote will come back at £14,900. Once the other side knows your limit, they have no reason to go below it.

If you're pushed for a number, give a target or a range anchored well inside your limit, never the limit itself.

## Write it down first

Set your reservation price before the conversation, when you're calm, and write it down. Under pressure, people move their limit to close a deal, and later regret it. If an offer crosses your line, the answer is no, unless something has genuinely changed, such as new information about value or a better alternative.

Knowing your walk-away point isn't about being difficult. It's what lets you say yes confidently when a deal is good, and no without panic when it isn't. (See [when to walk away](/guides/when-to-walk-away).)
