Rent feels like a fixed price, but it often isn't. Landlords care about more than the monthly figure: empty weeks, agent fees, wear and tear and the hassle of finding someone new all cost them money. That gives tenants more room than most realise.

## When you have leverage

- **The property has been empty for a while.** Check how long it's been listed. A flat advertised for weeks is costing its landlord money every day.
- **It's a quieter time of year.** Winter months usually see less demand than late summer.
- **You're renewing.** Finding a new tenant costs a landlord time, fees and empty weeks. A reliable existing tenant is worth a lot.
- **You're a strong applicant.** Good references, stable income and no pets or smoking reduce the landlord's risk.

## Know the market

Before you negotiate, look up similar properties in the same area: same number of bedrooms, similar condition and transport links. If comparable flats are listed for less, that's your strongest argument. It also gives you your [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna): the flat you'd take instead.

## What to offer in return

Negotiation works best when you trade. Things that cost you little but matter to a landlord include:

- **A longer tenancy**, which means fewer empty months for them.
- **A flexible move-in date** to fit their plans.
- **Taking the property as it is**, if the issues are cosmetic.
- **Strong references** and proof of income up front.

This is [integrative negotiation](/guides/integrative-vs-distributive-negotiation): finding things each side values differently.

## A script for a new let

> "I really like the flat and I'm ready to move quickly. Similar two-bed flats nearby are listed at around £1,450. Would the landlord consider £1,425 on an 18-month tenancy? I can provide references and proof of income today."

## A script for a renewal

> "I'd like to stay, and I've been a reliable tenant for two years. The proposed rise to £1,600 is above what similar flats nearby are listed for. Would the landlord consider £1,520 if I sign for another 12 months?"

## Keep it polite and in writing

Agents pass messages on, so be friendly and specific. Put your offer in an email so it reaches the landlord accurately, and keep a copy of anything agreed.

## The worst case

The worst common outcome is that the landlord says no and you're where you started. In a market where a good tenant saves a landlord real money, a polite, reasoned offer is usually worth making.
