Why does nobody do the group project? Why do teams settle for safe, mediocre plans when everyone privately wants something more ambitious? A 270-year-old story about hunting explains it.

## The story

In his *Discourse on Inequality* (1755), the philosopher Jean-Jacques Rousseau described two hunters.

- If both hunt a **stag** together, they catch it and both feast.
- A stag needs two people. If one hunter wanders off to catch a **rabbit**, the stag escapes and the one still hunting it gets nothing.
- A rabbit is small, but you can catch it alone, guaranteed.

## The dilemma

Both hunters prefer the stag. There's no temptation to cheat: if your partner hunts the stag, you'd rather hunt it too.

The problem is **trust**. If you doubt your partner will stick with it, the safe move is the rabbit. And if both doubt each other, both go home with rabbits, even though both would rather have shared the stag.

That's what makes the stag hunt different from the [prisoner's dilemma](/guides/prisoners-dilemma-examples). In the prisoner's dilemma, cheating is always tempting. In the stag hunt, cooperating is best, as long as you believe others will cooperate too. The game has two stable outcomes, and which one you end up in depends on expectations.

## Where it shows up

- **Group projects.** If you think nobody else will try, doing the minimum is the safe play.
- **Ambitious team goals.** A bold plan only works if everyone commits.
- **Switching technology.** A new system is better if everyone uses it, and useless if only you do.
- **Revolutions and protests.** Joining is safe only if enough others join.
- **Business partnerships.** Both sides invest only if they trust the other to invest too.

## How to get people to hunt the stag

1. **Make effort visible early.** When people see others committing, they commit too.
2. **Start with a small shared win.** Catching a small stag builds trust for a bigger one.
3. **Communicate clearly.** Unlike the prisoner's dilemma, talking really helps here, because nobody has a reason to lie about wanting the stag.
4. **Reduce the cost of being let down.** Backup plans and fair division of work make cooperation less risky.

The fix isn't more talent. It's more trust.

So, which one are you in a group project?
