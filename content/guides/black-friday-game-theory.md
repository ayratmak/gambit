Black Friday is a giant strategic game between shops, and between shops and you. Three of its favourite moves come straight out of game theory and behavioural economics.

## Move 1: "We'll match any price"

It sounds like the best deal on the high street. Economists have long pointed out that it can work the other way.

Picture two shops selling the same TV for £500. Without a price match, each is tempted to cut to £450 and steal the other's customers. That's a [prisoner's dilemma](/guides/prisoners-dilemma-examples): both cut, and both earn less.

Now one shop promises to match any lower price. If its rival cuts to £450, it simply matches, and the rival wins no customers. Cutting no longer pays, so neither shop cuts. **The promise to go low can keep prices high.** Economists including Steven Salop wrote about this in the 1980s, describing price-matching clauses as a way rivals can avoid competing without ever talking to each other.

That doesn't make price matches useless. If you check and claim them, you save money. But a shop that shouts loudest about matching isn't necessarily the one with the lowest prices.

## Move 2: "Was £199, now £129"

A crossed-out price is an anchor. Your brain compares £129 with £199 and registers a £70 win, even if the item has sold for £129 for most of the year. That's [anchoring](/guides/anchoring-in-negotiation), the same effect that makes first offers so powerful in negotiation.

UK consumer groups have repeatedly found Black Friday "deals" that were cheaper, or the same price, in the months before. The fix is simple: use a price-history tool or check the item a few weeks earlier, and judge the price on its own, not against the "was" figure.

## Move 3: The countdown timer

"Deal ends in 02:14:37" is a commitment device for you, not the shop. It pushes you to decide before you compare, which is exactly when you're weakest. In negotiation, this is called an [exploding offer](/guides/negotiation-tactics-and-counters), and the counter is the same: decide your maximum before you see the clock.

## How to play Black Friday well

1. **Make a list before the sales.** If it wasn't on your list, the deal is probably buying you.
2. **Set a maximum price for each item** based on what it normally sells for.
3. **Check price history**, not the "was" price.
4. **Use price matches**, but don't assume the matcher is cheapest.
5. **Ignore timers** on things you hadn't planned to buy.

Shops have spent years studying the game. A little game theory evens it up.
