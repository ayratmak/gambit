Game theory can sound like a subject for economists and mathematicians. In fact, its most useful ideas can be learned in an afternoon and used for a lifetime. Here's a roadmap for starting in the new year.

## Step 1: Learn to read a payoff table

Everything starts with a simple grid: your options down the side, the other player's along the top, and what each of you gets in each box. Once you can read one, you can analyse almost any simple game.

## Step 2: The prisoner's dilemma

Learn why two sensible people can both end up worse off. It's the single most important example in the subject. Start with our guide to the [prisoner's dilemma](/guides/prisoners-dilemma-examples).

## Step 3: Dominant strategies and Nash equilibrium

Learn to spot moves that are best whatever others do ([dominant strategies](/guides/dominant-strategy-explained)), and situations where nobody wants to change ([Nash equilibrium](/guides/nash-equilibrium-explained)).

## Step 4: Games played in turns

Learn [backward induction](/guides/backward-induction): solving a decision from the end and working back. The pirate game is a great puzzle to practise on.

## Step 5: Repeated games and cooperation

Learn how repetition changes everything, through Axelrod's tournaments and [tit for tat](/guides/tit-for-tat-strategy).

## Step 6: Commitment, signals and focal points

Learn why limiting your options can make you stronger ([the game of chicken](/guides/game-of-chicken)), why actions beat promises ([costly signals](/guides/signalling-game-theory)), and how people coordinate without talking ([focal points](/guides/schelling-focal-point)).

## Step 7: Apply it

Pick one area of your life and use the ideas:

- **Negotiation:** start with [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna) and [anchoring](/guides/anchoring-in-negotiation).
- **Business:** see [game theory in business](/guides/game-theory-in-business).
- **Auctions and buying:** read about the [winner's curse](/guides/winners-curse).

## Good resources

- **Books:** *Thinking Strategically* by Avinash Dixit and Barry Nalebuff is the friendliest introduction. *The Strategy of Conflict* by Thomas Schelling is a classic. (See our list of the [best negotiation books](/guides/best-negotiation-books).)
- **Practice:** play the games, don't just read about them.
