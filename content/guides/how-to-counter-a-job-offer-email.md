Email is a good way to counter a job offer. It lets you think, choose your words and make a clear case without being put on the spot. The trick is to sound enthusiastic, specific and reasonable all at once.

## Before you write

- **Know your number** and the lowest you'd accept, based on market data and your [alternatives](/guides/what-is-batna).
- **Pick one or two reasons** that matter to the employer: your experience, the scope of the role, market rates.
- **Decide what else you'd value** if salary can't move: start date, remote days, holiday, training, a signing bonus or an earlier review.

## Template 1: asking for a higher salary

> Subject: Re: Offer – [Role]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you again for the offer. I'm genuinely excited about the role and the team.
>
> Having looked at the full package, I'd like to ask whether there's flexibility on the base salary. Given the scope of the role, including [specific responsibility], and what similar roles are paying in [city], I was hoping for £[number].
>
> If we can get there, I'm ready to accept and could start on [date].
>
> Thanks for considering it. Happy to talk it through on a call if that's easier.
>
> Best wishes,
> [Your name]

## Template 2: when salary is fixed

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for explaining the salary band. I understand it's fixed.
>
> Would there be flexibility on a couple of other points? Specifically:
> – an extra remote day each week
> – a pay review at six months rather than twelve
>
> Either would make a real difference for me, and I'd be delighted to accept on that basis.
>
> Best wishes,
> [Your name]

## Template 3: accepting after a counter

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for coming back on this. I'm very happy to accept the offer at £[agreed number], with [any other agreed terms].
>
> Could you send the updated offer letter? I'm looking forward to starting on [date].
>
> Best wishes,
> [Your name]

## Tips on tone

- **Lead with enthusiasm.** They need to know you want the job.
- **Use a specific number.** Precise, reasoned figures are more persuasive than ranges, which invite them to pick the bottom. (See [anchoring](/guides/anchoring-in-negotiation).)
- **Give a reason that's about value**, not about your bills.
- **Make it easy to say yes.** "If we can get there, I'm ready to accept" removes their worry that you'll keep pushing.
- **Ask once.** Don't send follow-ups lowering your number before they've replied.

Once you've agreed, get the revised offer in writing before you resign from your current job.
