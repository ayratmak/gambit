Every deal happens in a gap. On one side is the most the buyer will pay. On the other is the least the seller will accept. If those two numbers overlap, there's a deal to be done. That overlap is the **ZOPA, the zone of possible agreement.**

## A simple example

You're selling a car.

- The least you'll accept is £7,000, because a dealer has offered you that. That's your [reservation price](/guides/reservation-price).
- The buyer will pay up to £8,000, because a similar car elsewhere costs £8,200 and they'd rather avoid the hassle.

The ZOPA runs from £7,000 to £8,000. Any price in that range leaves both of you better off than walking away. The negotiation is really about where in that £1,000 the deal lands.

## When there's no ZOPA

If you need £7,000 and the buyer's limit is £6,500, there's no overlap. No amount of clever talk will close it on price alone. You have three options:

1. **Add value.** Include the winter tyres, offer to deliver, or be flexible on timing. Things that are cheap for you but useful to them can open a zone that wasn't there.
2. **Change the alternatives.** If the buyer learns that comparable cars cost more than they thought, their limit moves.
3. **Walk away.** Sometimes the honest answer is that there's no deal. Knowing that early saves everyone time. (See [when to walk away](/guides/when-to-walk-away).)

## How to find the ZOPA

You know your own limit. You don't know theirs, which is the whole game. To estimate it:

- **Research their [alternatives](/guides/what-is-batna).** What would they do without you, and what would it cost them?
- **Ask open questions.** "What are you comparing this with?" or "What's driving your timeline?" reveal more than you'd expect.
- **Watch their concessions.** Big early moves mean there's room. Small, slow moves mean you're near their limit.

## How to land near your end of the zone

The deal usually lands closer to the side that:

- **Anchors well.** A confident, well-reasoned [first offer](/guides/first-offer-in-negotiation) pulls the final price towards it.
- **Has the better alternative.** If you can walk away, you can wait.
- **Is more patient.** The side in a hurry tends to give up more of the zone.
- **Concedes slowly.** Small, shrinking concessions signal that you're near your limit.

## The mistake to avoid

The biggest ZOPA mistake is revealing your limit. "My budget is £8,000" tells the seller exactly where the top of the zone is, and that's where the price will go. Keep your reservation price private, and let the other side reveal theirs first.
