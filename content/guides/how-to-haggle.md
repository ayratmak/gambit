Haggling feels un-British. That's exactly why it works: most people don't do it, so shops, tradespeople and service providers often have room to move that nobody asks for.

## Where haggling works in the UK

- **Big-ticket items:** sofas, beds, kitchens, TVs and appliances.
- **Ex-display and end-of-line stock**, where the shop wants the space.
- **Independent shops and markets.**
- **Tradespeople**, especially for larger jobs or flexible timing.
- **Contracts:** broadband, mobile, insurance and TV, particularly at renewal.
- **Cars**, new or used. (See our guide to [negotiating a car price](/guides/how-to-negotiate-car-price).)

## 9 tactics that work

1. **Ask the magic question.** "Is that the best you can do?" Then stop talking. Silence does a lot of the work.
2. **Do your research first.** A competitor's price on your phone is far more persuasive than "it's a bit expensive".
3. **Ask for a reason to discount.** "Is there any flexibility for ex-display?" or "Do you price match?" gives staff an easy way to say yes.
4. **Bundle.** Buying the bed and the mattress? Ask for a deal on both, or free delivery and removal of the old one.
5. **Offer something in return.** Paying in full today, collecting yourself or being flexible on installation dates can all be worth something to them.
6. **Use a precise counter.** "Could you do £847?" sounds considered. Round numbers invite round cuts. (Here's why [anchoring](/guides/anchoring-in-negotiation) works.)
7. **Talk to the right person.** Junior staff often can't discount. Ask politely whether a manager could help.
8. **Be willing to leave.** If there's a similar product elsewhere, you can afford to walk, and people can tell.
9. **Stay friendly.** People give discounts to customers they like. Rudeness gets a firm "no".

## Haggling over the phone

For broadband, mobile and insurance, the best deals usually come from the retention team, who handle customers thinking of leaving. Before you call:

- check what new customers are being offered
- get a competitor's quote
- know the date your contract ends

Then say you're thinking of leaving and ask what they can do. If the first offer isn't good enough, it's fine to say you'll think about it and call back.

## When not to bother

Haggling over a £3 item in a supermarket chain wastes everyone's time. Save it for purchases where a 5 to 15% saving is real money, or where the seller clearly has room to move.

## The mindset

Haggling isn't confrontation. It's a polite question with an obvious answer the seller is allowed to give. The worst outcome is "no", and you're no worse off than if you hadn't asked.
