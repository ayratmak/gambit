A house is the biggest thing most people ever negotiate. In England and Wales, a sale isn't legally binding until contracts are exchanged, so there's often room to negotiate both before and after your offer is accepted.

## 1. Research what homes actually sold for

Asking prices are hopes. Sold prices are facts. Look up recent sold prices for similar homes nearby, which are published from Land Registry records, and adjust for size, condition and features. This gives you an objective basis for your offer.

## 2. Find out the seller's position

Ask the agent politely:

- How long has it been on the market?
- Has the price been reduced?
- Has the seller found somewhere to buy?
- Why are they selling?

A seller who's found their next home and is worried about losing it has a weaker [alternative](/guides/what-is-batna) than one who's in no hurry.

## 3. Make your offer strong, not just low

Sellers care about certainty as much as price. Before you offer, get:

- a mortgage agreement in principle
- a solicitor ready to act
- your own sale under offer, or say you're chain-free if you are

Then make a specific, reasoned offer:

> "We'd like to offer £312,500. Similar three-beds on the road have sold for £305,000 to £318,000 in the past year, and this one needs a new kitchen. We're chain-free with a mortgage agreed in principle and can move as quickly as the seller needs."

A precise figure with reasons is a stronger [anchor](/guides/anchoring-in-negotiation) than a round one.

## 4. Handle "best and final" carefully

If several buyers are interested, the agent may ask for sealed best and final offers. This is where buyers overpay, because the winner tends to be the most optimistic bidder: the [winner's curse](/guides/winners-curse). Decide your absolute limit beforehand, and don't stretch on the day.

## 5. Renegotiate after the survey if needed

If the survey finds problems such as damp, roof repairs or electrics that need work, get quotes and put a reasoned case to the agent:

> "The survey found the roof needs repairs quoted at £6,800. We'd like to reduce our offer by that amount."

Be factual, not opportunistic. Sellers accept genuine issues far more readily than a last-minute squeeze.

## 6. Be willing to walk away

There will always be another house. Buyers who'd be happy with two or three properties negotiate far better than those who've fallen in love with one.
