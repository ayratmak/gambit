Most negotiations are won or lost before anyone speaks. Experienced negotiators spend more time preparing than talking. Here are ten questions to answer before your next one, whether it's a pay rise, a supplier contract or a car.

## About you

**1. What do I want, and why?**
Write down the outcome you'd love and the reasons behind it. The reasons matter, because there may be other ways to meet them.

**2. What's my alternative if there's no deal?**
This is your [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna). If you can't name it, your first job is to build one.

**3. What's my walk-away point?**
The worst deal you'd accept, based on your alternative. This is your [reservation price](/guides/reservation-price). Write it down.

**4. What am I willing to trade?**
List things that are cheap for you but valuable to them: timing, volume, flexibility, references, a longer contract.

## About them

**5. What do they want, and why?**
Their stated position ("£40,000 maximum") usually hides interests ("we can't break the salary band"). Interests are where solutions hide.

**6. What's their alternative?**
What happens to them if you walk away? If their alternative is weak, you have more power than you think.

**7. What's their walk-away point, roughly?**
Estimate it from research and comparable deals. Between your limit and theirs is the [ZOPA](/guides/zopa-negotiation).

## About the conversation

**8. What's my opening offer, and how will I justify it?**
Ambitious but defensible, ideally precise, with one or two clear reasons. (See [should you make the first offer?](/guides/first-offer-in-negotiation))

**9. What objective criteria can we use?**
Market rates, published prices, comparable deals and expert valuations take the heat out of disagreements.

**10. What will I say if they push back?**
Prepare answers to the three most likely objections, and plan your concessions so each one is smaller than the last.

## One page is enough

You don't need a long document. One page answering these ten questions, written before the meeting, will make you calmer and harder to push around. Most people who feel they "lost" a negotiation simply walked in without one.
