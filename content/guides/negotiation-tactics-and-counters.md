Most pressure tactics work for the same reason: they push you to decide before you've compared your options. Once you can name them, they lose most of their power.

## 1. The nibble

**What it is:** The deal is agreed. Then: "Oh, one more thing. You'll include delivery, won't you?" Saying no feels petty, so people give in.

**Counter:** Answer a nibble with a nibble. "Sure, if you can pay the deposit today." Or put a price on it: "Delivery is £60."

## 2. Good cop, bad cop

**What it is:** One person plays tough, then a friendlier colleague steps in with a "better" offer. A 1993 study by Joseph Hilty and Peter Carnevale found a tough-then-nice sequence won more concessions than the reverse.

**Counter:** Judge the offer against your [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna), not against the bad cop's. Ask yourself: is this offer good, or just better?

## 3. The exploding offer

**What it is:** "This price is only valid until Friday." The deadline stops you shopping around.

**Counter:** Ask politely for more time, and ask what changes after the deadline. A deadline that bends tells you they want the deal.

## 4. The flinch

**What it is:** A visible, pained reaction to your number, designed to make you doubt it.

**Counter:** Don't fill the silence with a concession. Restate your reasoning calmly.

## 5. The extreme anchor

**What it is:** An opening offer so high or low that every counter feels reasonable by comparison.

**Counter:** Don't counter from it. Name it as unrealistic and put your own reasoned number on the table. (More in our guide to [anchoring](/guides/anchoring-in-negotiation).)

## 6. Higher authority

**What it is:** "I'd love to, but my manager won't approve it." It lets them say no without being the bad guy, and gives them a second round later.

**Counter:** Before you start, ask, "Are you able to agree this today?" If not, keep your best concessions back for the person who can.

## 7. Splitting the difference

**What it is:** "Let's just meet in the middle." It sounds fair, but the middle depends on where each side started.

**Counter:** Check where the middle actually is. If they opened high, the middle is theirs. (See our guide on [focal points](/guides/schelling-focal-point).)

## 8. Silence

**What it is:** They go quiet after your offer, hoping you'll fill the gap with a better one.

**Counter:** Wait. Or ask, "What are you thinking?" Never answer silence with a concession.

## 9. Salami slicing

**What it is:** Small requests, one at a time, each too small to refuse, that add up to a big change.

**Counter:** Ask for the whole list before you agree to anything. "What else is on your mind?"

## The common thread

Every tactic on this list is designed to get you to judge an offer against the moment, not against your alternatives. If you know your walk-away point before you walk in, very few of them work.
