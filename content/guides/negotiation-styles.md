Everyone has a default way of handling conflict. Some people dig in, some give way, some go quiet. In the 1970s, Kenneth Thomas and Ralph Kilmann mapped these defaults into five styles, based on two questions: how much you push for your own goals (assertiveness), and how much you care about the other side's (cooperativeness).

## 1. Competing: "I win"

High assertiveness, low cooperation. You push hard for your outcome.

**Works when:** it's a one-off deal, like buying a used car from a stranger, or when you must protect something important.
**Risk:** damages relationships and misses joint gains.

## 2. Collaborating: "We both win"

High assertiveness, high cooperation. You look for a deal that fully meets both sides' interests.

**Works when:** the deal is important and the relationship matters, like a long-term supplier contract or a job offer.
**Risk:** takes time and needs trust on both sides. (See [integrative negotiation](/guides/integrative-vs-distributive-negotiation).)

## 3. Compromising: "Let's meet in the middle"

Medium on both. Each side gives something up.

**Works when:** time is short and the stakes are moderate.
**Risk:** splitting the difference can leave value on the table and reward whoever opened most extreme.

## 4. Avoiding: "Let's not"

Low on both. You sidestep the issue.

**Works when:** the issue is trivial, emotions are running high, or you need more information.
**Risk:** problems don't disappear, and the other side may decide for you.

## 5. Accommodating: "You win"

Low assertiveness, high cooperation. You give the other side what they want.

**Works when:** the issue matters far more to them than to you, or you want to build goodwill.
**Risk:** if it's your habit, people learn to expect it.

## Which one are you?

Think about the last three times you disagreed with someone over money or time. Did you dig in, give way, split it, avoid it or work it through together? Most people lean heavily on one or two styles.

## The skill is switching

Good negotiators aren't stuck in one style. They choose based on the stakes and the relationship:

- **High stakes, long relationship:** collaborate.
- **High stakes, one-off deal:** compete, politely.
- **Low stakes, long relationship:** accommodate or compromise.
- **Low stakes, one-off:** avoid, or don't bother negotiating at all.

Knowing your default is the first step. Choosing on purpose is the second.
