Car prices are among the most negotiable things you'll ever buy. A little preparation can save you hundreds, sometimes more.

## Before you go

- **Research comparable prices.** Look up the same make, model, age and mileage on several sites. Note the cheapest real listings.
- **Know your alternative.** Have at least one other car you'd be happy with. That's your [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna), and it's what lets you walk out.
- **Get your part-exchange value separately.** Online valuations and instant-buyer quotes give you a benchmark so a dealer can't hide a discount inside a low trade-in offer.
- **Arrange finance in advance** if you need it, so you can compare the dealer's deal with your own.

## Timing

Dealers often work to monthly and quarterly sales targets, so the last days of a month or quarter can bring better offers. In the UK, March and September are the big months, when new number plates come in. The months just before them can be good times to buy outgoing stock.

## At the dealer

1. **Agree the price of the car first.** Keep part-exchange, finance and extras separate, or discounts can quietly move from one to another.
2. **Make a precise, reasoned offer.** "Similar cars are listed at £14,200 and £14,450, so I can do £14,150 today" is far stronger than "Can you do any better?"
3. **Use silence.** Once you've made your offer, wait for their reply.
4. **Expect the "manager" round.** The salesperson may "check with the manager". That's normal. Hold your number.
5. **Be ready to leave.** Saying "Thanks, I'll think about it" and walking out often brings a call back.

## Ask for extras

If the price won't move, ask for things that cost the dealer less than they're worth to you:

- a full tank of fuel
- a service or a new MOT
- a longer warranty
- mats, winter tyres or a towbar
- a better price for your part-exchange

## Buying privately

Private sellers usually have less room but more motivation, especially if they've already bought their next car. Ask how long it's been for sale and why they're selling. Point out issues politely, back them up with a mechanic's report or the MOT history, and make a specific offer.

## The one rule

Never fall in love with a car on the forecourt. The buyer who's happy to walk away gets the best price.
