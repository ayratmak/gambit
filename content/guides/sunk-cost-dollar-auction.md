Here's a game the economist Martin Shubik designed in 1971. It has made many people pay more than £20 for a £20 note.

## The rules

A £20 note is auctioned to a room. Bids go up in £1 steps. The highest bidder wins the note. The twist: **the second-highest bidder also pays their bid, and gets nothing.**

## What happens

Bidding starts cheerfully. £1, £2, £5. Why not? A bargain.

Then two people are left, say at £18 and £19. The person on £18 thinks: if I stop now, I lose £18. If I bid £20, I might win the note and break even. So they bid £20.

Now the person on £19 faces the same logic. Stop and lose £19, or bid £21 to win a £20 note and lose only £1. They bid £21.

And on it goes. Each step is rational on its own. Together, they're a disaster. In classroom versions, bidding regularly ends well above the note's value.

## The sunk cost trap

The dollar auction is a sharp example of the sunk cost fallacy: letting money you've already spent drive what you do next. The £18 you've bid is gone either way. The only question is whether the next bid is worth it, and in an escalating war it rarely is.

## Where it happens in real life

- **Bidding wars on houses**, where pride and effort spent push offers past what the property is worth. (See the [winner's curse](/guides/winners-curse).)
- **Legal disputes** where costs grow larger than the amount in dispute.
- **Failing projects** that keep getting funded because so much has already been spent.
- **Price wars** that neither side wants to be first to stop.

## How to avoid it

1. **Set your limit before you start**, and write it down. Your [walk-away point](/guides/when-to-walk-away) is easiest to keep when you chose it with a cool head.
2. **Judge the next step only on what comes next.** "If I were starting fresh today, would I pay this?"
3. **Look for the escalation pattern.** If both sides are committing more just to avoid losing what they've spent, step back.
4. **Make a way out.** Offering the other side a face-saving exit can end a war that neither wants.

The cheapest way to win a dollar auction is not to enter it.
