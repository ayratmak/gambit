Most negotiations go wrong in predictable ways. Here are the ten most common mistakes and what to do instead.

## 1. Not preparing

Walking in without knowing your [alternative](/guides/what-is-batna) and walk-away point is the root of most bad deals. Twenty minutes with a [preparation checklist](/guides/negotiation-preparation-checklist) fixes this.

## 2. Accepting the first offer

First offers usually leave room. Accepting instantly can even make the other side feel they offered too much. Thank them and ask for time.

## 3. Revealing your limit

"My budget is £15,000" tells the other side exactly where to land. Keep your [reservation price](/guides/reservation-price) private.

## 4. Negotiating against yourself

You make an offer, they go quiet, and you lower it before they've said a word. Make your offer, then wait.

## 5. Splitting the difference automatically

The middle depends on where each side started. If they opened high, splitting the difference hands them a win. Check where the middle is first.

## 6. Focusing only on price

Price is often the hardest thing to move. Timing, payment terms, extras and length of contract can be worth more and cost the other side less.

## 7. Conceding without trading

Every concession should get something back. "I can do that if you can..." keeps the deal balanced.

## 8. Making concessions too fast

Big, quick concessions signal there's plenty more to come. Make them smaller and slower as you go.

## 9. Letting emotion drive decisions

Anger leads to walk-outs you regret. Desperation leads to deals you regret. If you feel either, take a break.

## 10. Not getting it in writing

Verbal agreements get remembered differently. Confirm the key terms by email straight after you agree.

## The pattern

Almost every mistake on this list comes from the same place: judging an offer against the moment instead of against your alternatives. Know your walk-away point, trade rather than give, and give yourself time to think. That alone puts you ahead of most people across the table.
