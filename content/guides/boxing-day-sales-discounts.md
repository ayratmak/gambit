The Boxing Day and January sales are the closest thing the UK has to a national haggling season. Prices are already down, stores want to clear stock, and staff often have more room to move than usual. Here's how to get the most from it.

## 1. Check the real price, not the "was" price

A crossed-out price is an [anchor](/guides/anchoring-in-negotiation), designed to make the sale price feel like a win. Before you buy, check what the item actually sold for in recent months using a price-history tool or by looking at other retailers.

## 2. Ask about ex-display and end-of-line stock

Display models, last-of-line items and boxes with damaged packaging are often the most negotiable things in the shop. Ask:

> "Is there any extra discount on the display model?"

## 3. Bundle your purchases

Buying a sofa and a footstool, or a TV and a soundbar? Ask for a deal on the lot, or for free delivery and installation. Shops would rather give a discount than lose a multi-item sale.

## 4. Ask for a price adjustment

Bought something in December that's now cheaper? Some retailers will refund the difference within a set period. Others won't, but you can often return the item, if it's unused and within the returns window, and buy it again at the lower price.

## 5. Use competitor prices

If another retailer has the same item cheaper, show the price on your phone and ask whether they'll match it. Many big retailers have formal price-match policies with conditions, so ask what theirs are.

## 6. Ask the simple question

On larger items, a polite "Is that the best you can do?" followed by silence still works. Ask a manager if the person serving you can't authorise a discount. (More tactics in [how to haggle](/guides/how-to-haggle).)

## 7. Know your limit before you walk in

Sales are designed to make you buy things you hadn't planned. Make a list, set a maximum price for each item, and leave the rest. The best bargain is the thing you were going to buy anyway, for less.
