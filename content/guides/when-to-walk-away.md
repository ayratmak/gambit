The ability to walk away is the most underrated skill in negotiation. Not because walking away is good, but because being *willing* to changes everything about how you negotiate.

## Five signs it's time to walk

1. **The best offer is worse than your alternative.** If the deal on the table is below your [reservation price](/guides/reservation-price), any agreement leaves you worse off than your [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna).
2. **They won't negotiate in good faith.** Moving goalposts, reneging on agreed points or refusing to share basic information all suggest the deal won't hold.
3. **You're only staying because of what you've invested.** Time and effort already spent are sunk costs. If the next step isn't worth it on its own, stop. (See the [dollar auction](/guides/sunk-cost-dollar-auction).)
4. **The risks outweigh the price.** Unlimited liability, unrealistic deadlines or terms you can't meet can make a "good" price a bad deal.
5. **Your gut says no and your notes agree.** Feelings alone aren't enough, but if your preparation and your instinct both point the same way, trust them.

## Why willingness to walk helps

When the other side senses you need the deal, they hold firm. When they believe you're comfortable leaving, they start making offers. That's why building a strong alternative before you negotiate matters so much.

But never fake it. A bluff that gets called leaves you with less credibility and a worse deal.

## How to walk away well

- **Stay calm and polite.** Drama burns bridges you may want later.
- **Be brief and clear.** "Thanks for your time. At this price it doesn't work for us, so we'll leave it here."
- **Leave the door open.** "If anything changes on your side, I'd be glad to hear from you."
- **Don't keep negotiating on the way out.** A walk-away that turns into another round isn't credible.

## What often happens next

Sometimes nothing, and that's fine: you've avoided a bad deal. But often the other side comes back, because your exit has told them where your real limit is. If they do, judge the new offer against your alternative, not against the relief of being back at the table.

The best negotiators don't walk away often. They just always know they could.
