Every January, millions of people make plans that their February selves ignore. Game theory has a surprisingly useful way to think about this: treat your future self as another player, one with different priorities.

## The problem: two selves

Today's you wants to save, exercise and eat well. February's you, tired on a cold evening, wants the sofa and a takeaway. Economists call this **present bias**: rewards now feel much bigger than rewards later.

You can't simply trust your future self to follow the plan. You need to change their options.

## The solution: commitment devices

A commitment device is a choice you make now that limits what your future self can do, or makes breaking the plan costly.

The oldest example is in Homer's *Odyssey*. Ulysses wants to hear the sirens' song, which lures sailors to their deaths. So he has his crew tie him to the mast and plug their ears with wax. He knows his future self will beg to be released, so he removes the option.

Another famous example, often told as strategy: when Hernán Cortés landed in Mexico in 1519, he had most of his ships scuttled. With no easy way home, his men had only one option left.

## Why it works: the game theory

Thomas Schelling, who shared the 2005 Nobel prize in economics, showed that **limiting your own options can make you stronger**. A negotiator who can't go above a limit, or a driver who's thrown away the steering wheel in the [game of chicken](/guides/game-of-chicken), is more believable than one who can change their mind. The same logic works on yourself.

## Commitment devices that work

1. **Automate it.** A savings transfer on payday beats deciding to save each month.
2. **Pay in advance.** A prepaid course or class makes skipping costly.
3. **Make it public.** Telling friends adds a reputational cost to quitting. (See [costly signals](/guides/signalling-game-theory).)
4. **Put money on it.** Deposit money with a friend that you lose if you fail.
5. **Remove the temptation.** Don't keep biscuits in the house. Delete the app.
6. **Make it easy.** Lay out your gym kit the night before.

## The takeaway

Don't rely on willpower in February. Use January to change the game your future self will play.

Happy new year.
