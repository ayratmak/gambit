It's Christmas Eve, and somewhere a group of colleagues is about to play the white elephant gift swap, also called a Yankee swap or "steal the present". It looks like a silly party game. It's actually a neat lesson in game theory.

## The rules (one common version)

1. Everyone brings a wrapped gift and draws a number.
2. Player 1 opens a gift.
3. Each following player either **opens** a new gift or **steals** an opened one.
4. Anyone who's stolen from opens a new gift or steals from someone else (usually not the gift just taken from them).
5. A gift is often "frozen" after it's been stolen two or three times.

## Going last is a big advantage

Player 1 opens blind and is likely to lose whatever they get. The last player sees every opened gift and can steal the best one that's still available. It's the same logic as the glass bridge in *Squid Game*: **later players benefit from information that earlier players paid for.** Some groups let player 1 take a final turn at the end to make up for it.

## When to steal and when to open

The decision is simple in principle: compare the best gift you can see with what you'd expect from an unopened box.

- **Early on**, there's little to steal, and an unopened gift has decent odds. Open.
- **Later**, the opened gifts include the best ones and the unopened ones are probably average. Steal.
- **Watch the freeze rule.** A gift on its last steal is the one to take, because once you have it, nobody can take it back.

## The strategic steal

Sometimes you should steal a gift you don't want most, because taking it forces its owner to steal something else, which can free up the gift you really want later. It's a small exercise in thinking several moves ahead, the essence of [backward induction](/guides/backward-induction).

## What to bring

- **Popular, useful gifts** get stolen and passed around, which makes the game fun.
- **Joke gifts** get laughs but rarely get kept, so if you draw a late number, don't count on anyone stealing yours.

## The real lesson

The gift swap shows something that holds in business and life: being first is often worse than it looks, information has value, and the best move depends on what everyone else has already done.

Merry Christmas, and good luck with your number.
