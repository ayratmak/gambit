Anyone can say they're the best candidate, the most reliable supplier or the most committed buyer. Saying it costs nothing, so it doesn't convince anyone. Game theorists call this **cheap talk**. What does convince people is a **costly signal**: an action that would be too expensive to fake.

## The peacock's tail

A peacock's tail is heavy, conspicuous and makes it easier for predators to catch him. That's exactly why peahens pay attention to it. Only a healthy, strong bird can afford such a handicap. In 1975 the biologist Amotz Zahavi called this the **handicap principle**.

## Degrees and job markets

In 1973 the economist Michael Spence used the same logic to explain education in job markets. Part of what a degree tells an employer is not what you learned, but that you could complete something long and demanding. It works as a signal because it's harder for less able or less persistent candidates to get. Spence shared the 2001 Nobel prize in economics for this work.

## Signals in business

- **Long warranties.** A five-year warranty is believable because it would bankrupt a firm selling unreliable products.
- **Money-back guarantees.** They cost nothing if the product is good.
- **Expensive shopfronts and advertising.** They suggest a firm expects to be around long enough to earn the money back.
- **Founders investing their own money.** Investors trust founders who have something to lose.

## Signals in negotiation

The same principle tells you how to be believed.

- **Not credible:** "I have other offers."
- **Credible:** "Here's the written offer from the other company."
- **Not credible:** "We're serious buyers."
- **Credible:** "We've had a mortgage agreed in principle and can exchange in four weeks."
- **Not credible:** "This is our final offer."
- **Credible:** a public commitment that would be embarrassing to break.

Commitment strategies in the [game of chicken](/guides/game-of-chicken) work for the same reason: they're believable because they're costly to undo.

## Reading signals from others

Turn the logic around when you're on the receiving end. Ask: would this be expensive for them to fake? If a claim costs nothing, discount it. If it's backed by money, time or reputation, take it seriously.

Words are cheap. Make yours expensive.
