Some decisions happen in turns: I move, you respond, I respond to that. The best way to solve them is often to **start at the end and work backwards.** Game theorists call this backward induction.

## A simple example

You're deciding whether to make a cheeky offer on a house. The seller can accept, reject or counter. If they counter, you can accept or walk away.

Start at the end. If they counter, would you accept their likely counter? If yes, you know your cheeky offer has a safety net. If no, think about whether the cheeky offer risks losing a house you want. Working backwards turns a vague worry into a clear choice.

## The pirate game

The classic puzzle shows how powerful (and strange) backward induction can be.

Five pirates, ranked by seniority, must split 100 gold coins. The most senior proposes a split. Everyone votes. If at least half agree, it's done. If not, the proposer walks the plank and the next pirate proposes. Pirates want to survive first, then get as much gold as possible.

Work backwards from two pirates left: the senior one keeps everything, because their own vote is half. With three pirates, the proposer only needs one extra vote, so they bribe the pirate who'd get nothing in the two-pirate case with a single coin. Keep going, and the answer for five pirates is surprising: **the captain keeps 98 coins** and gives one coin each to two pirates who'd otherwise get nothing.

## Where it breaks down

In the centipede game, two players take turns either grabbing a growing pot or passing it on. Backward induction says the first player should grab immediately. In experiments, most people pass for several rounds and both end up richer. People trust a little, and it often pays.

So backward induction tells you what perfectly logical players would do. Real people don't always behave that way, which is worth knowing too.

## Using it in negotiation

1. **Map the moves.** What will you offer, how might they respond, and what will you do next?
2. **Start at the end.** What happens if there's no deal? That's your [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna).
3. **Work back.** Which opening gives you the best path through their likely responses?
4. **Plan your concessions in advance** so you're not deciding under pressure.

Thinking three moves ahead isn't a gift. It's a habit, and backward induction is how you build it.
