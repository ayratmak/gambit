Would you accept £1 out of £100? The ultimatum game asks exactly that, and the answer reveals something important about negotiation.

## The rules

Two players, £100.

- The **proposer** offers a split, say £70 for them and £30 for you.
- The **responder** can **accept**, and both get paid as proposed, or **reject**, and both get nothing.

It's played once, with no chance to haggle.

## What logic predicts

A purely self-interested responder should accept anything above zero. £1 beats £0. Knowing that, the proposer should offer £1 and keep £99.

## What people actually do

The economists Werner Güth, Rolf Schmittberger and Bernd Schwarze published the first ultimatum game experiments in 1982. Since then it's been run thousands of times around the world, and the results are strikingly consistent:

- **Offers below about 20% get rejected roughly half the time.** People pay real money to punish an unfair split.
- **Most proposers offer 40 to 50%.** They know a low offer is risky.

So the "irrational" behaviour of responders changes the rational move for proposers. Fairness isn't just a feeling. It works like a threat.

## Why people reject free money

Several things are going on:

- **Fairness norms.** People care about how a deal is split, not just what they get.
- **Punishment.** Rejecting an insulting offer costs the proposer more than it costs you.
- **Reputation instincts.** In daily life we usually meet people again, and being known as a pushover is costly.

## What it means for negotiation

1. **The other side's "rational" limit isn't their real limit.** An offer that's technically better than nothing can still be rejected if it feels insulting.
2. **Perceived fairness moves the [ZOPA](/guides/zopa-negotiation).** Explaining why your offer is fair, with objective criteria, makes it easier to accept.
3. **Leaving something on the table can be smart.** In ongoing relationships with colleagues, suppliers or landlords, a split that feels fair now buys goodwill later.
4. **Take-it-or-leave-it offers are risky.** They turn a negotiation into an ultimatum game, and people do say no.

So, what's the lowest you'd accept out of £100? Most people's honest answer is well above £1.
