Auctions sell everything from houses and art to oil rights and mobile phone spectrum. How an auction is designed changes how people should bid, and how much the seller earns. That's the subject of auction theory.

## The four classic auctions

**1. English auction (rising price).** Bids go up in public until one bidder is left. Think art houses and car auctions. The best strategy is simple: stay in until the price reaches your value, then stop.

**2. Dutch auction (falling price).** The price starts high and falls until someone shouts "mine". The Dutch flower auctions made it famous. You have to decide in advance how low to let the price fall, balancing a bargain against the risk someone else jumps first.

**3. First-price sealed bid.** Everyone submits one secret bid; the highest wins and pays what they bid. Many tenders and some property sales work like this. The best strategy is to bid *below* your true value, but by how much depends on how many rivals you face.

**4. Second-price sealed bid (Vickrey auction).** Everyone submits one secret bid; the highest wins but pays the **second-highest** bid. The economist William Vickrey analysed it in 1961, work that contributed to his 1996 Nobel prize.

## Why Vickrey auctions reward honesty

In a second-price auction, bidding your true value is a [dominant strategy](/guides/dominant-strategy-explained). Bidding higher risks winning at a price above your value. Bidding lower risks losing something you'd have happily paid for. Your bid only decides *whether* you win, not what you pay.

eBay's proxy bidding works much like this: you enter your maximum, and you pay a small increment above the next-highest bid.

## The winner's curse

When an item is worth roughly the same to everyone but nobody knows exactly how much, such as oil under the seabed, the winner tends to be the person who overestimated most. That's the [winner's curse](/guides/winners-curse), and the fix is to shade your bid below your estimate.

## The 2020 Nobel prize

Paul Milgrom and Robert Wilson won the 2020 Nobel prize in economics for improving auction theory and inventing new formats. Their work helped design spectrum auctions that governments have used to sell radio frequencies to mobile operators.

## How to bid well, whatever the format

1. **Decide your value before you start**, and write it down.
2. **Know the format**, because it changes the right strategy.
3. **Shade your bid** when the value is uncertain and there are many bidders.
4. **Don't chase sunk costs.** (See the [dollar auction](/guides/sunk-cost-dollar-auction).)
