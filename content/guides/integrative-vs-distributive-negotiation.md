There are two kinds of negotiation, and most people only practise one.

## Distributive: splitting a fixed pie

In **distributive** negotiation, there's a fixed amount to share, usually money. Every pound you gain is a pound they lose. Buying a used car from a stranger is mostly distributive.

The skills here are about claiming value: knowing your [BATNA](/guides/what-is-batna), [anchoring](/guides/anchoring-in-negotiation) well, and conceding slowly.

## Integrative: growing the pie

In **integrative** negotiation, you look for ways to make both sides better off. It works because people value things differently.

The classic example is two sisters arguing over one orange. They split it in half. One squeezes her half for juice and throws away the peel. The other grates the peel for a cake and throws away the fruit. Had they asked *why* each wanted the orange, both could have had everything they needed.

## Logrolling: trading what's cheap for what's valuable

The main integrative tool is **logrolling**: trading on issues you each care about differently.

Take a job offer where the salary is stuck at £45,000. Instead of fighting over it, add issues:

- **Start date:** you'd like six weeks; they're relaxed. Cheap for them.
- **Remote work:** worth a lot to you; costs them nothing.
- **Training budget:** valuable to you; small for them.
- **Salary:** important to both.

Concede where they care more, gain where you care more, and both sides end up better off than a straight fight over salary.

## How to switch from distributive to integrative

1. **Ask why.** "What makes the deadline important?" or "What's driving the budget?" uncovers interests.
2. **Add issues.** Price, timing, volume, risk, payment terms and quality all have different values to each side.
3. **Make packages, not single concessions.** "If you can do X, I can do Y."
4. **Share some information.** Integrative deals need trust. Start small and see if it's returned.

## Don't forget to claim

Growing the pie isn't the same as being soft. Once you've created value, you still need to make sure you get a fair share of it. The best negotiators do both: they [grow the pie](/guides/zero-sum-vs-positive-sum), then divide it well.
